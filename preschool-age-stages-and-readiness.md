
## Preschool Age Stages in India: A Quick Guide

| Stage | Typical Age | What It Usually Involves |
|---|---|---|
| Play School or Playgroup | About 1.5 to 3 years | Short sessions, play, songs, and getting used to time away from parents |
| Nursery | About 3 years | First formal preschool class, with learning through play |
| LKG | About 4 years | More structured activities and early reading and number readiness |
| UKG | About 5 years | Activities that prepare children for Class 1 |
| Class 1 | About 6 years | Beginning of formal primary education |

*Note: These are typical age ranges. Actual admission age requirements may vary by school and state.*

## What Does the National Education Policy Say?

India's National Education Policy (NEP) 2020 defines ages 3 to 8 as the **Foundational Stage**. It includes three years of preschool education for children aged 3 to 6, followed by Classes 1 and 2.

The policy recommends play-based and activity-based learning during these early years. It also identifies age 6 as the starting point for Class 1.

In practice, a child who starts nursery around age 3 may progress through Nursery, LKG, and UKG before entering Class 1 at around age 6.

However, NEP 2020 does not mean every child must start preschool on their third birthday. Parents should check the latest admission age criteria of their chosen school.

## What Are the Signs Your Child Is Ready for Preschool?

Every child develops at a different pace. Look for these common signs of readiness. Your child does not need to demonstrate all of them before starting preschool.

- Can stay with a familiar adult for a short time without parents.
- Shows interest in other children, even from a distance.
- Can communicate basic needs through words, gestures, or expressions.
- Has a reasonably regular sleep and meal routine.
- Can follow simple instructions such as "come here" or "wash your hands".
- Shows curiosity about new places, toys, and activities.

### Is Toilet Training Necessary Before Preschool?

Some schools expect children to be toilet trained, while others provide support during the learning process. Ask the school about its toilet-training policy before admission.

Avoid rushing your child. Readiness, patience, and a supportive environment are more important than meeting a fixed deadline.

## Final Thought

Choosing the right preschool age involves more than following an age chart. Consider your child's comfort, communication, curiosity, and ability to adjust to a new environment.
