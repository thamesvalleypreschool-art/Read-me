
## Is Earlier Always Better?

No. Starting preschool early does not guarantee a head start, and starting later is not a failure. A child who joins at three with a warm teacher and a gentle routine can do just as well as one who joined at two.

The quality of the school and how comfortable the child feels matter more than the start date.

There is one important exception. If both parents work long hours, childcare may be needed before preschool age. This is a daycare decision. Whatever the child's age, look for a safe, caring environment with a clear daily routine.

## What If Your Child Isn't Ready Yet?

If your child seems unsure about starting preschool, consider these three simple options:

1. **Wait a few months:** Give your child more time to develop confidence and comfort with new surroundings.

2. **Start with Short Days:** Ask whether the school allows shorter sessions or a gradual settling-in period.

3. **Practise at Home:** Arrange short play sessions with other children, visit the school's playground, and talk about preschool in a positive and cheerful way.

If your child is still struggling to settle after several weeks, speak with the teacher. Sometimes the challenge may be related to age or readiness. In other cases, the school environment or adjustment routine may need attention.

## Final Thought

Starting preschool is an individual journey. Give your child time, choose a supportive learning environment, and focus on emotional comfort rather than comparing their age with other children.
